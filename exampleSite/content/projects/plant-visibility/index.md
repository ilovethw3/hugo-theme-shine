## The challenge

Westfield Knox is a 128,000 m² regional shopping centre in Melbourne's eastern suburbs, with annual electricity consumption of 18 GWh. The centre had completed an 820 kWp rooftop solar installation in 2021, but without storage, only 31% of solar generation was consumed on-site — the remainder was exported at the low Victorian feed-in tariff of AUD 0.05/kWh, while grid electricity was purchased at peak rates of AUD 0.35/kWh during evening retail hours.

The challenge was to increase solar self-consumption while also managing the centre's large evening peak demand, which occurred after solar generation had ceased for the day.

## The solution

NexVolt designed a 500 kWh battery system using the NV-BESS 500, integrated with the existing SMA solar inverters via the NV-Hybrid 100K AC-coupled interface. The system was installed in a purpose-built switchroom adjacent to the main electrical substation.

NV-EMS Cloud was configured with three simultaneous dispatch objectives:
1. **Solar self-consumption:** Absorb surplus solar generation during the day
2. **TOU arbitrage:** Charge from grid during off-peak tariff periods overnight
3. **Peak shaving:** Discharge during the evening retail peak (16:00–21:00)

The AI dispatch engine balances all three objectives in real time, using 24-hour solar generation and load forecasts to optimise the daily charge/discharge schedule.

## Results after 12 months

The solar self-consumption rate increased from 31% to 84%, with the battery absorbing surplus midday solar generation and delivering it during the evening peak. The combined effect of higher self-consumption, peak shaving, and overnight TOU charging reduced grid electricity costs by 42% — an annual saving of AUD 1.26 million against a system cost of AUD 1.8 million, delivering a payback period of under 5 years.
