## The challenge

Bavarian Precision Components (BPC) operates a CNC machining facility in Munich producing structural components for three major European automotive OEMs. The facility runs two production shifts per day, five days a week, with a peak electrical demand of 850 kW during machining cycles.

BPC's electricity tariff includes a demand charge component based on the highest 15-minute demand reading each month. Because production scheduling varied week to week, demand peaks were difficult to predict or control — resulting in demand charges that ranged from €28,000 to €41,000 per month, representing 44% of the total electricity bill.

## The solution

NexVolt designed a 1 MWh battery storage system using two NV-BESS 500 cabinets, managed by NV-EMS Cloud. The system was installed in an existing electrical room adjacent to the main switchboard, requiring no structural modifications to the production facility.

NV-EMS Cloud analysed BPC's historical interval meter data to identify the typical demand profile and peak timing patterns. Within the first week of operation, the AI dispatch algorithm had learned the production schedule and was pre-charging the battery in anticipation of peak machining periods.

| System component | Specification |
|---|---|
| Battery storage | 2 × NV-BESS 500 (1 MWh total) |
| Power output | 500 kW continuous |
| Inverter | NV-Hybrid 100K |
| Management | NV-EMS Cloud Professional |
| Grid connection | 10 kV, 1.25 MVA |

## Delivery and commissioning

Installation was completed over a single weekend shutdown to avoid disrupting production. NexVolt's commissioning team validated island mode operation, demand response integration with the local grid operator, and remote monitoring connectivity before handing over to BPC's facilities team.

## Outcomes after 12 months

The system consistently maintained BPC's monthly demand peak below 530 kW — a 38% reduction from the pre-installation average of 850 kW. Annual demand charge savings of €142,000 were supplemented by an additional €34,000 from time-of-use arbitrage and demand response revenue.

> "We were sceptical that a software-managed battery could reliably handle the variability in our production schedule. After 12 months, the system has exceeded our expectations — both in savings and in reliability."
> — Head of Facilities, Bavarian Precision Components GmbH
